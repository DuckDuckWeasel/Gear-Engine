# Game Concept and Creative Brief

## The Decision

Choose one identity route before production brand, UI/UX, store, press, pitch, and trailer assets proceed.

| Route | Strategic posture | Best quality | Primary risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Gear Engine** | Evolve the current project name | Most explicit product-mechanic fit | Sounds technical and may feel like middleware |
| **B — Cog Runner** | Evolve the imported UI name | Friendliest and most immediately mobile | Existing marketplace phrase use and “runner” genre ambiguity |
| **C — Clockwork Apex** | Introduce a new consumer-facing name | Strongest premium tactical-racing position | Longer name; “Apex” is crowded in games and needs deeper clearance |

**Recommended for production exploration: Route A — Gear Engine.** It owns the actual differentiator—the player-engineered gear system—without implying an endless runner or borrowing the crowded competitive language of “Apex.” The wordmark and symbol must make it feel like a game, not an engine product.

## Verified Product Core

The player builds a mechanical system rather than directly tuning a list of car statistics. Gear placement and relationships create consequences during a race. The race then produces results, rewards, and roguelike choices that change the next configuration cycle.

**Core loop:** configure → race → observe cause and effect → earn → choose an upgrade → reconfigure.

**Player fantasy:** become the mechanic-strategist whose machine wins before and during the race.

**Short promise:** build a smarter machine, then watch it prove itself at speed.

## Audience and Positioning

Primary audience: mobile players who enjoy optimization, deckbuilding or roguelike choice, readable systems, short-session progression, and stylized racing. Secondary audience: puzzle and automation players attracted by chain reactions and visible causality.

Positioning territory: **tactical racing through mechanical composition**. The identity should avoid presenting the game as a conventional steering racer, an idle simulator, or a pure engineering sandbox.

## Route A — Gear Engine

![Gear Engine generated concept board](<file:///Users/leonardosilva/Documents/MatheusCohen/Gear Engine/Artifacts/Marketing/Evidence/GeneratedConcepts/GearEngineRouteBoard.png>)

![Gear Engine route mark](<file:///Users/leonardosilva/Documents/MatheusCohen/Gear Engine/Artifacts/Marketing/BrandExploration/GearEngineRouteMark512.png>)

### Positioning

**Category statement:** a tactical mobile racing game where the machine you build determines how the race unfolds.

**Promise:** engineer the chain; own the race.

**Tone:** precise, kinetic, resourceful, bold, competitive without aggression.

**Visual thesis:** an engineered motorsport workshop translated into graphic, high-contrast mobile entertainment. Gears are active cause-and-effect devices, not background decoration.

**Symbol idea:** a mechanical gear interrupted by a racing line. The interruption prevents a generic settings-icon silhouette and creates an ownable movement cue.

**Palette:** Graphite `#0D1117`, Warm Ivory `#F4F0E6`, Signal Orange `#FF6B35`, Electric Cyan `#33C8FF`, Steel `#65717E`.

**UI mood:** dark instrument panel, warm paper-like cards, cyan system feedback, orange primary action. Mechanical state should be legible before decoration.

**Icon sketch:** orange gear plus cyan racing line on graphite. At 48 px, the ring and diagonal line remain the only required details.

**Store thumbnail test:** strongest contrast and clearest link to the verified gear board. The potential weakness is settings-app association; the racing-line cut and orange/cyan motion must remain unmistakable.

### Typography Directions

1. **Angular engineered display:** custom-cut Bricolage Grotesque for titles; Hanken Grotesk for UI; JetBrains Mono for telemetry.
2. **Condensed motorsport:** a narrow squared display face paired with Hanken Grotesk; strongest speed signal, weaker friendliness.
3. **Wide mechanical wordmark:** extended uppercase proportions with circular counters derived from the gear; most ownable, needs custom drawing.
4. **Mixed-case workshop:** sturdy mixed-case grotesk with one altered `G`; most approachable, least overtly competitive.

### Naming Risk

Descriptive and easy to understand, but broad web search is crowded by literal engines, game engines, and mechanical products. Domain and trademark availability are unverified. A distinct wordmark and descriptor such as “Tactical Racing” may be needed during discovery, not necessarily in the public title.

## Route B — Cog Runner

![Cog Runner generated concept board](<file:///Users/leonardosilva/Documents/MatheusCohen/Gear Engine/Artifacts/Marketing/Evidence/GeneratedConcepts/CogRunnerRouteBoard.png>)

![Cog Runner route mark](<file:///Users/leonardosilva/Documents/MatheusCohen/Gear Engine/Artifacts/Marketing/BrandExploration/CogRunnerRouteMark512.png>)

### Positioning

**Category statement:** a playful mobile gear-building racer where every snapped-in part changes the run.

**Promise:** snap it together; send it flying.

**Tone:** optimistic, tactile, clever, mischievous, welcoming.

**Visual thesis:** toy-racing energy and friendly modular construction. Parts should look satisfying to place, connect, and activate.

**Symbol idea:** an interlocking two-gear cluster accelerated by a forward arrow. Rounded teeth and warm color reduce the industrial feel.

**Palette:** Cream `#FFF7E8`, Sunny Yellow `#FFBD2E`, Coral `#FF5A54`, Mint `#55C9B5`, Charcoal `#2B3138`.

**UI mood:** bright cream fields, chunky cards, large tactile targets, animated snap feedback, minimal chrome.

**Icon sketch:** yellow and mint interlocking cogs with a coral forward cue. It reads as a toy system at small size.

**Store thumbnail test:** the friendliest route and closest to the imported UI reference. It may understate strategic depth and can be mistaken for an endless runner.

### Typography Directions

1. **Rounded geometric display:** broad soft terminals and circular counters; warm and app-friendly.
2. **Playful variable grotesk:** Bricolage Grotesque at wide width with selective bounce; energetic without becoming juvenile.
3. **Compact sticker lettering:** custom high-weight uppercase forms; strongest icon/decal integration, lower long-copy suitability.
4. **Friendly mixed-case system:** Hanken Grotesk with a custom cog-shaped `o`; most scalable and readable, least distinctive without the custom glyph.

### Naming Risk

An unrelated App Store product, Lunibopix, already uses “Cog Runner” as a mini-game name. The phrase is therefore provisional and requires deeper store, trademark, domain, and legal review. “Runner” may also set the wrong genre expectation if steering and endless movement are not the dominant interaction.

## Route C — Clockwork Apex

![Clockwork Apex generated concept board](<file:///Users/leonardosilva/Documents/MatheusCohen/Gear Engine/Artifacts/Marketing/Evidence/GeneratedConcepts/ClockworkApexRouteBoard.png>)

![Clockwork Apex route mark](<file:///Users/leonardosilva/Documents/MatheusCohen/Gear Engine/Artifacts/Marketing/BrandExploration/ClockworkApexRouteMark512.png>)

### Positioning

**Category statement:** a precision tactical racer where clockwork chains reshape every lap.

**Promise:** build the line before you break the clock.

**Tone:** focused, premium, exact, dramatic, high-skill.

**Visual thesis:** a racing laboratory in which luminous mechanical chains bend the optimal line. Controlled technical glow replaces conventional neon overload.

**Symbol idea:** a clockwork ring crossed by an apex-shaped racing line. The central node represents the decisive mechanical link.

**Palette:** Midnight `#081126`, Acid Lime `#D6FF2F`, Hot Magenta `#FF2D9D`, Cool White `#EAF0F8`, Titanium `#71798A`.

**UI mood:** navy field, strict data rhythm, luminous causal links, confident negative space, bright state changes reserved for consequences.

**Icon sketch:** segmented clockwork ring plus magenta apex. It has the strongest competitive signal and the least literal car read.

**Store thumbnail test:** distinctive in a dark icon grid and suitable for premium presentation. It risks looking like a generic competitive or rhythm title without a secondary racing cue.

### Typography Directions

1. **Precision extended grotesk:** wide uppercase forms, technical spacing, restrained cuts.
2. **Condensed competition display:** narrow caps for leaderboards and performance headlines; powerful but familiar.
3. **Custom clockwork monogram:** angular `C` and `A` built from ring segments; most ownable, requires careful small-size drawing.
4. **Editorial contrast:** technical display face with Hanken Grotesk body and JetBrains Mono telemetry; strongest pitch-deck sophistication.

### Naming Risk

The exact phrase produced no obvious Google Play, App Store, Steam, or general game-title result in the preliminary search, but both component words are crowded. “Apex” strongly overlaps established competitive-game language, including Apex Legends associations. This is not legal clearance.

## Production Invariants

Whichever route is selected:

- A gear must function as a cause-and-effect symbol, not a settings glyph.
- Racing energy must be visible without promising direct-control features that are not verified.
- The board/loadout must remain the product’s visual center of gravity.
- UI hierarchy must work at portrait mobile size with accessible contrast and touch targets.
- Generated art remains exploratory until approved elements are reconstructed deterministically.
- Store screenshots must use reproducible gameplay, not route-board compositions.

## Approval Gate

Select **Route A — Gear Engine**, **Route B — Cog Runner**, or **Route C — Clockwork Apex**. A hybrid is possible only if its retained name, palette, symbol logic, and tone are specified before production begins.

After selection, production proceeds with the brand kit, vector logo library, icon suite, UI/UX rebrand specification, Google Play package, press kit, player presentation, publisher pitch, investor overview, and trailer animatic.
